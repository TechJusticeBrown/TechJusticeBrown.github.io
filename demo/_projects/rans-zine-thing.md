# Tech + Ideology Zine Circle

Hi ziners! I had an idea to do a reading group / zine circle where we spend a few weeks, or an even shorter block of time ("zine sprint") engaging critically with technology and ideology's co-construction. The reason is I feel that texts and course offerings on tech x humanities often give survey views instead of narrowing down and diving deep, and I also want to create more structure for low-stakes project work (i.e. something that can fit on a single page in a zine) :)

Somehow, a number of my awesome friends were interested, so here is a doc systemizing my current planning so we can forge forward together!

**What do you get?**

- Free book and/or free food! (see "funding" section below.)
- Very manageable time commitment. Minimal required readings between sessions, 1 person gets 1 page in the zine, and sessions will either take place once a week (1.5 hrs weekly; 4-6 weeks) OR in a more concentrated block of time (zine sprint; a few days of intensive reading/discussion/zine-ing and a period afterwards to wrap up & print the zines).
- Structured space for project work, and a very cool final project as bite-sized as you want it to be :)

**Notes on format:**

- **Why zine & not traditional reading group?&#32;**It's cool to read theory and then get your hands dirty (by coding, zine-ing, blog posting...). And when people make & share imperfect, creative artifacts as they grapple w/ theory in real-time, ideas thrive, remain live and accessible, and stay in productive flux! School sometimes doesn't allow fun, low-stakes knowledge production, but we'd rather have this than having the shame of creating/theorizing imperfectly preventing us from trying out daring ideas at all.
- **Rooting each zine circle in only&#32;*one*&#32;core text:&#32;**this is up for discussion, but it's an approach I appreciate. It's rare that classes allow us to spend several weeks on a whole book (even 1 essay!) or a single analytic framework (even one we disagree with), but it's a good feeling, and productive, and also a respite.
- **We're still a reading group** in that text is engaged with - but you have something to show for it and it's an experiment in alternative knowledge production!

## ZINE CIRCLE #1

### Zine-ing the political economy of affect in the age of AI

*A zine sprint on technocracy, affect, and GenAI (or: networked media)&#32;*

- ***Affect****&#32;is the conditioned emotional reactions we have towards concepts & cultural objects.&#32;*
- *We read a small set of theorists who draw upon Marxism & psychoanalysis to analyze how technocracy wields&#32;**affect**&#32;to control us. (An idea is to root the zine in a core text - Mark Fisher's Capitalist Realism (+ Zizek's interpassivity), or Walter Benjamin's "Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction", or another text from the Frankfurt school?)*
- *We put these ideas - developed to understand the role of technology & affect in the rise of fascism at earlier points in history - in conversation w/ modern AI. How does it help us understand AI, AI-mediated art, & the cultural imaginaries that technologies & interfaces smuggle into our lives? Where does it fall short and become outdated? As STEM students and people who use their agency to create things, how do we use our knowledge of code/design/writing/science to design interventions, or design towards them?*

## ZINE CIRCLE #2

### Privacy, Decentralization, and Social Organization **Beyond the Cypherpunk Manifesto**

*Another idea for a zine sprint!*

The Cypherpunk movement's work ([manifesto](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/cypherpunk-manifesto/) here), rooted in a philosophy that digital privacy is a fundamental right, has been instrumental to the existence of decentralized, privacy-preserving technologies that, today, both empower communities and make new forms of social organization possible.

But the Cypherpunk movement is also deeply libertarian! How can we reconcile its original philosophy and the array of tools it has given us with other movements and ideologies, including those with radically different ethos? What can and can't be reconciled?

Here are some of my starting questions. I'm sure you have your own that excite you!

- Privacy-preserving and decentralized technology may empower individuals, but communities fighting for a different kind of social organization must *organize themselves first.&#32;*How do they do so, with this technology as an intentionally-wielded tool and not the organizing force itself? What are theories of change from the cipherpunk movement, and others? (Cybersyn, Twin Cities)
- Critics of libertarianism point out that market deregulation comes at great environmental cost, but so do some forms of decentralizing technology, such as blockchains! When we hear of grassroots projects that use blockchains for a social justice mission (e.g. Bread Cooperative, Nina Protocol), to what extent should we wonder whether their theories of change abstract out other factors that are also necessary to liberation? (e.g. racial + environmental justice and a disentanglement from capitalist finances.)
- What can we learn from reading the 1993 Cypherpunk manifesto in conversation with other*&#32;*manifestos and position statements?
- Outside of technology, what are cultural and environmental logics of federation and decentralization that converse productively with cypherpunk and/or social organization beyond it? How might embodiment and metaphor fit in, e.g. by acting as narrative intervention? (Le Guin, Harroway, mycelial networks, your own sensory hankerings.)

#### **Blockchain, social organization, & theories of change**

*I'm not avidly interested in blockchains, but this module exists because I feel their trustless nature makes them a site of unique affordances where theories of change have been imagined and re-imagined many times, but to me, there exists no satisfying analysis (esp. a Marxist one) synthesizing and responding to more leftist takes on crypto & the blockchain.*

*In this section, my selected readings reflect my subjective (!) view that it's dangerous to see decentralized technology as inherently liberatory or utopic; liberation is incoherent if it doesn't account for environmental justice; it's tricky to make sweeping claims about crypto without recognizing different implementations have different impacts. But your thoughts may be different!*

***Theory:***

- [But how does bitcoin actually work?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bBC-nXj3Ng4) - 3Blue1Brown
- Resource on the environmental costs of crypto. 
    - (proof of work vs. proof of stake).
- Excerpt from *The Revolution will Not be Funded,*
    - A collection of essays written by organizers that offer cautionary perspectives against radical political organizing that is materially dependent on elite financing. Interesting in conversation w/ "A Revolution for the Perplexed".

***Artifacts:***

- [**The Bread Cooperative Manifesto](https://docs.bread.coop/about/manifesto/)&#32;**
    - Workers building decentralized financial tools for each other & those in need. Run cooperative-style.
- [**Why the Cooperative Movement Should Care About Crypto Still](https://platform.coop/blog/why-the-cooperative-movement-should-care-about-crypto-still/)&#32;- Joshua Dávila - 2026**
    - Dávila created the Bread Cooperative & runs the blog [The Blockchain Socialist](https://theblockchainsocialist.com/), which espouses crypto in its socialist theory of change.
- [**A Revolution for the Perplexed](https://theluddite.org/post/blockchain-radicals.html)&#32;- The Luddite - 2025**
    - A book review. Argues that blockchains aren't really a useful tool in socialist organizing, Dávila's theory of change is misguided, and that the Bread Cooperative has a material basis on capitalist finances, and thus reproduces capitalist relations while labeling them post-capitalist.
- **The Nina Protocol**
    - A now-defunct music distribution protocol that aimed to allow artists to distribute music and retain 100% of profits via the blockchain's decentralized logic. In my understanding, it went defunct because, despite its utopic ethos, it had an incoherent revenue model.
- **Excerpt from&#32;*Blockchain Chicken Farm*&#32;- Xiaowei Wang - 2020**
    - Not really grassroots but interesting. Digitally-verifiable food safety in rural China.
